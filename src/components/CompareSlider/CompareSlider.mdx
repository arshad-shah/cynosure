# CompareSlider

Two versions of the same thing, one over the other, split by a divider people move to compare them.

<Canvas of={Stories.BeforeAndAfterSplitByADivider} />

## When to use

- **Before and after** of an image edit, a redesign or a render, where the difference is in the detail.

**Use something else when:**
- people pick a number: [Slider](?path=/docs/components-controls-slider--docs)
- two panes are both worked in: [SplitPane](?path=/docs/components-structure-splitpane--docs)

## Usage

```tsx
import { CompareSlider } from "@arshad-shah/cynosure";

<CompareSlider beforeSrc={original} beforeAlt="Original" afterSrc={edited} afterAlt="Edited" />
<CompareSlider before={<canvas ref={left} />} after={<canvas ref={right} />} />
```

## One box for both sides

The two sides share one box: the shape of the first picture, video or canvas (the before side's, else the after side's), as wide as the container. `beforeSrc` and `afterSrc` (each with its alt text) draw the pictures for you; two pictures of different sizes, or even shapes, are both contained in the box, centred, so they always line up. Any other child is stretched to the box; a Cynosure [Image](?path=/docs/components-feedback-and-display-image--docs) lines up when given the same `ratio` and `fit="contain"`. With no media at all the sides lay out in the flow and the taller sets the height.

Control the divider with `value` and `onChange` (percent of the width showing `before`).

## Zoom and pan

`zoom` takes the same settings as DocumentViewport, so [ZoomControls](?path=/docs/components-structure-zoomcontrols--docs) drives it: `{ kind: "fit-page" }` (the default) shows the whole box, and `{ kind: "percent", value: 100 }` is actual size, one picture pixel per CSS pixel. Both sides scale together and the frame keeps its size. Larger than the frame, the sides pan: drag the picture, use the wheel, or Tab to the view and use the arrows. A press on the divider's line still drags the divider. `zoomable` adds Ctrl/Cmd+wheel, pinch and Ctrl/Cmd with = - 0.

```tsx
const [zoom, setZoom] = useState<ZoomSetting>({ kind: "fit-page" });
const [scale, setScale] = useState(1);

<ZoomControls zoom={zoom} percent={scale * 100} onZoomChange={setZoom} />
<CompareSlider
  beforeSrc={original}
  beforeAlt="Original"
  afterSrc={optimised}
  afterAlt="Optimised"
  zoom={zoom}
  onZoomChange={setZoom}
  onScaleChange={setScale}
  zoomable
/>
```

## Behaviour and accessibility

- **Role:** the divider is a slider named "Comparison", read as "40% before". Rename it and the tags with `labels`.
- **Pointer:** at fit, press anywhere on the picture to jump the divider there (and focus it), then drag; Escape mid-drag puts it back. Zoomed past the frame, a press on the picture pans and a press on the divider's line drags the divider.
- **Zoomed view:** while it pans, the frame is a tab stop (a group named "Zoomed comparison") before the divider; its arrows pan 40px, 200px with Shift. The wheel pans it too; at fit the wheel scrolls the page. A new zoom setting is announced ("Zoom 100%").
- **Zoom keys** (`zoomable`): Ctrl/Cmd and =, - or 0 zoom in, out and to 100% while focus is inside; Ctrl/Cmd+wheel and pinches zoom around the pointer.
- **Keyboard:** arrows move 1%, Shift+arrows and Page Up/Down 10%, Home and End go to the ends.
- **Touch:** at fit, a sideways drag moves the divider at once and a tap jumps it; an upward or downward swipe scrolls the page. Zoomed past the frame, a finger pans the picture (`touch-action: none`) and two fingers pinch (`zoomable`). The divider's hit area is 44px on touch screens.
